Audit Methodology & Prop 13 Commercial-to-Residential Mechanics

1. Dual-Dataset Audit Architecture

Residential property tax capacity comes from U.S. Census ACS 5-Year uncapped aggregate residential real estate taxes paid (B25090_E001 ÷ B01003_E001) rather than top-coded median taxes (B25103). Business vitality comes from U.S. Census ZIP Code Business Patterns (ZBP, 2014–2023) tracking private-sector establishments (est), jobs (emp), and annual payroll (ap).

2. Why Audit Gaps Happen Under Prop 13

Healthy Bay Area communities often show Dual Engine compounding: commercial payroll growth attracts high-earning residents, housing turnover resets Prop 13 assessments (otherwise capped at 2%/yr), and residential tax capacity rises. An Audit Gap occurs when a ZIP holds dense or fast-growing business payroll (e.g., long-held commercial/industrial campuses under Prop 13 or commuter office hubs with scarce housing supply) while per-capita residential tax growth trails Bay Area medians.

3. Data Hygiene, Suppression & Updates

Census ZBP withholds exact payroll/employment (ap_nf=‘D’) where a single dominant employer could be identified (such as Cupertino 95014 in 2014); suppressed fields are kept null rather than coerced to zero. Also note the 2019 ACS questionnaire redesign to exact dollar write-ins (toggle Recent YoY or 3-Yr for post-redesign momentum).
